To go along with the "anyone can cook" attitude is also the reality that "anyone can make mistakes". And whew, if you thought I was some kind of culinary goddess where every dish I touch is highly edible, you're so very wrong.

Last night I attempted to make a vegetarian shepherd's pie. Well, I chose to use beef stock because that's what I had open in the fridge (and I haven't found a palatable vegetable stock yet), but it could be easily made vegetarian. I had already done all the chopping the day before while I had made dinner, so was looking forward to an easy cook. Ha. Ha.

Mistake one: recipe called for a can of lentils. Never having used canned lentils and having several types on hand, I thought I'd just cook some myself. Turns out my usual inability to cook lentils or beans reared its ugly head here. True story: I either overcook or undercook all beans and lentils, making lentil salad and chilli made with anything other than canned (pre cooked) beans impossible. First I realized -too late-- that the type of lentil I thought I would use needed to be soaked in water for a few hours before cooking. Okay, I thought, I can use a different kind, which cook up rather quickly. However, they also apparently turn to mush: as soon as I added them to the rest of the vegetables for the filling of the pie, they disintegrated, creating a lentil-mash speckled with mushrooms, carrots and onions, instead of the sort of lentil-salad-looking filling it was supposed to be.

What we ended up having for dinner was mashed lentils with veg with a broiled mashed potato topping. Now, it did still smell good, as all of the flavour was correct and such, but the texture was that of baby food which isn't really what we adults are after in our dinners most nights. Win some, lose some I suppose. It was at least edible.

I messed with the recipe from Canadian Living so much, that I don't mind posting what I did here for anyone interested. Just know that I cannot advise you on how to cook the lentils properly; if you find out how, do tell.

**Vegetarian Shepherd's Pie (GF too)** You will need a 6 cup/1.5L casserole dish for this. Something deep works better than shallow (I didn't have the right size and had to borrow one from my mum!).

- 1 cup dried lentils, cooked (recipe was 1 540mL can, drained and rinsed)
- 3/4-1 cup broth
- 1 tbsp cornstarch (for gluten free, OR use flour; I used neither thickener)
- 2 large russet/baking potatoes (~700g if you have a scale)
- 1 package mushrooms (227g), finely chopped
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 1 carrot, finely chopped
- 1/2 tsp dried thyme
- 1/4 cup chopped fresh parsley (optional)
- ~1 cup buttermilk
- (I'm going to assume that you have butter, olive oil, salt and pepper handy)

Cook lentils. You could chop all the vegetables at once in a food processor if you prefer; I did them by hand. Prick potatoes all over with a fork and microwave on high 8-10 minutes, set aside to cool (I cut them in half to speed the process). Whisk cornstarch into broth OR if you're me, use a Smitten Kitchen hack and mix flour into equal parts softened butter; set aside.

In a large skillet or pot, heat a glug of olive oil on medium high, add carrots, thyme, and onions, cook for a couple minutes. Add mushrooms and a good pinch of salt and cook until there is little to no liquid left in the pan. Stir in lentils--I would turn down the heat a little here--and cook a minute more. (If you're me, you don't cook any longer since the lentils are already mush. This also resulted in me not needing any kind of thickener and using more broth). Add broth mixture to the pot, and flour mixture if using instead, stir well and cook until thickened. Stir in about half the parsley. Pour/scrape into 6 cup/1.5L casserole dish.

Scoop potato out of their skins into a bowl and mash (with fork or masher) with ~1 tbsp butter, and 1/2 cup buttermilk to start as well as salt and pepper to taste. Add more buttermilk (or butter if you like) as needed until potatoes are no longer dry. Stir in remaining parsley, then spread over the lentil and veg mixture in your casserole dish. Dot with butter and broil in oven for 5-10 minutes, just until top is golden (recipe suggested 3-5 minutes, but my oven took way longer to begin browning the potatoes).
