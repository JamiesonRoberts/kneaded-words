_“And that’s my new philosophy!” - You’re a Good Man Charlie Brown (the musical)_

My ultimate goal here is to help my fellow food-lovers eat well without breaking the bank. So first I must explain: what do I mean by eating well? For me, eating well is using whole foods to cook delicious meals at home. If you don’t like cooking, stay with me here, because I’m not talking gourmet. Delicious, as far as I’m concerned is simply “flavourful”. That frozen dinner you bought on sale for .99 cents sure was cheap, but it’s not exactly flavourful, it’s just salty. And on the other end of the scale, that $22 entree sure was delicious, but it wasn’t exactly cheap. I want to find the middle ground.

There’s four parts to my philosophy in achieve this: (“Listicle” or not, it’s true!)

1. **Cooking at home.** The best way to save on food (no not the BC grocery chain) is to cook at home. Yes, this requires a time investment, but more on that later. You’ve probably seen articles comparing the price of a fresh salad from McDonalds (or the UCC cafeteria, at least when I was there) to what you would spend on its ingredients. The research is in: eating out is [expensive](http://lifehacker.com/how-i-broke-my-addiction-to-takeout-and-started-cooking-476039932). So cooking at home is your first step
2. **Buy what you need.** Food waste is a pretty painful [reality](http://globalnews.ca/news/2075857/food-waste-at-record-levels-as-other-canadians-go-hungry/). And while some people try to eliminate food waste for environmental reasons, waste is also relevant to your wallet. It may be a painful paradigm, but seeing dollar bills when you’re trashing that food you didn’t get around to eating will motivate you to cut back on waste.
3. **Meal planning.** The best way to avoid waste is to plan your meals. This is not as daunting as it seems, as it actually saves time to plan ahead a little, but more on that later. If you know what you’re going to eat over the week, you can buy only what you need. Stick to the plan and you’ll use up all the food you bought, with little to no waste.
4. **Buying smart.** Price match. Pick your produce carefully. Buy what’s on special. These are all ways of buying smart when you’re in the grocery store. Going in with a plan helps, but you can also plan around sales by browsing flyers easily accessible online or through an app (such as [flipp](https://www.flipp.com)) for extra savings. It doesn’t seem like much per item, but you might be surprised how it adds up.

Google any of these topics and you’ll find lots of helpful articles on how to go about achieving them, but I’m going to write about it all right here in one place for you, with special attention for the locals in my city. Food cost and availability is extremely regional so, while there are some generalities that hold true almost everywhere, there’s no one-size fits all when you're getting down to specifics. So, “Stay tuned!” as they say, for details on how I go about incorporating each of those four philosophies into my daily life to eat well on a budget. I promise that it’s not nearly as hard as you might think.
