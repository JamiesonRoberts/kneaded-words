My husband often marvels at me in the kitchen, and for a while I wasn’t sure why. It turns out that through practice, as mentioned, you gain a lot of otherwise unnoticed skills and intuition when you’re cooking. As a short cut for the studious want-to-be-cook, here are a few things he’s pointed out to me that make me extra efficient in the kitchen.

- **Prep ingredients before hand.** Also, for health reasons, try to leave any kind of meat prep for last, ideally on a separate cutting board and space from your other ingredients. Your recipe will dictate how many extra bowls this might need (or if you can leave it all on the cutting board). To save on dishes, combine ingredients that will be cooked at the same time. Onions are usually the first thing to be cooked, so try to leave their prep for last so you can just scrape them off of the board and into the pan/pot.
- **Clean up as you go.** Something simmering in the pan for a few minutes? Wash any items you are finished with or load them in the dish washer. Wiping down the counter wouldn’t go amiss either. Dish all done and in the oven baking? Wash up those dishes! Don’t leave it until after you’ve eaten: you won’t want to clean up then, trust me.
- **Set a timer.** I don’t really need to do this anymore, as inevitably I end up checking my timer right before it’s about to go off: you develop a bit of a sixth sense for certain amounts of time as you go. However, setting a timer means I don’t have to worry about over-doing something while I’m busy doing something else (thus it is especially useful for not over cooking of pasta), whether that’s cleaning up or the following tip.
- **Prepping between steps.** This is a super cheat and not for the faint of heart as you have to have confidence. As mentioned in my chilli recipe, if you know you’ve got time between steps, you can use it to prep the next ingredient. The key here is to read the recipe ahead, spot the time where you’ll be waiting for something to cook, then, knowing roughly how much time the prep will take, decide to use that time to prep something instead of doing it all before hand.

I think that's about it, but I will add to this list if I find any more quirky ways to make things easier in the kitchen!
