Before we dive in to my next segment (on meal planning), how about a little something to break things up?

There are some things that I always have on hand but I never buy unless they’re on sale. As you might have guessed, these are all non perishables as a result. How much I buy depends on how much room I have in the budget that week, although usually what I’m buying is cheap enough (1$/item) that it doesn’t make a huge dent even if I stock up. When I wasn’t operating on a cash budget, and for things like shampoo that tend to go on sale less often, I do use a credit card.

I don’t just buy this stuff on sale for a mere 30-cents-off; I try to buy when I’m saving at least a dollar, if not more, depending on the product. For some items, I’ll buy just two, for others I’ll buy five or six. It depends on what you’re buying and how long it keeps: for example, mayonnaise - I’ve discovered - doesn’t keep forever so it’s best to only have one backup. If that. Butter, however, can go in the freezer which means I can always bake on a whim.

The minimum pre-tax price I usually wait for is listed after the product:

natural and regular peanut butter, $4 butter, $3 canned tomatoes or beans, $1 jarred tomato sauce, $1 dried pasta, $1 soup stock (chicken, beef, or vegetable), $1-1.50 cheese, $5/500g bar (the non fancy types) crackers, like wheat thins or fish crackers, $1.50-2 prepared canned soups, $1-2 depending on the brand condensed soups, >$1 rice cakes, ~$1 each (sometimes 4/$5 type of deal) frozen spinach pizzas, $3, or roughly 50% off junky treats like Oreos, unknown, but that’s just how rarely I buy them

There's also a handful of fresh things I will only buy on sale, like "fresh" tortellini or ravioli. As I'm browsing for the flyers that week, I work them into my meal plan. I also try to buy frozen fruit and vegetables on sale, but if I run out before hand, I'll pay full price. What kinds of things do you only buy when they're on sale?

Recipe time! With fall upon us here in London, ON, the cool weather has arrived. No doubt they'll be a few heatwaves to come before we get that frigid chill that sets in for a couple of months without the snow. But still: I think it's time for chilli. You can make this for about 8$ for the bulk of the ingredients, with the assumption you have some spices on hand. It's filling, serves at least four, and usually results in freezer-friendly leftovers.

This recipe is based around Smitten Kitchen's [vegan](http://smittenkitchen.com/blog/2007/01/hibernation-fare/) version as well as friends' suggestions, personal preferences, and happy experiments. I have yet to succeed at cooking dried beans, but you can follow SK's cooking instructions in [this version](http://smittenkitchen.com/blog/2014/04/three-bean-chili/) if you want to go that route. My recipe card for this reads "Ever Evolving Chili" as it's rarely the same twice, whether that's because I have an old bell pepper that needs using, or whether I have ground beef that week. I highly recommend roasting some fresh tomatoes (and/or bell pepper) in the oven to add--drizzle with a good amount of oil, salt and pepper, maybe even oregano, and roast at 325 F until they're starting to brown and burn at the edges. You could also easily use other ground meats (like turkey). Have fun and make your own "Evolving" Chilli! This recipe is gluten- and dairy-free as it is, and if you don't use beef, it's also vegan (so long as you check your cocoa powder doesn't say "may contain dairy", the horror).

Ever Evolving Chili (serves 4-6, depending on level of hunger)

- 2-3 medium onions, finely chopped
- 1 x 15oz can each of kidney, black and pinto beans, drained and rinsed (I can only find "romano" beans canned, but these appear similar to pinto. It might be some kind of regional naming thing here.)
- 2 x 28oz cans diced tomatoes
- 1 small package of beef, 1-2lbs depending on preference (optional, and not included in price estimate)
- 2 heaping tbsp chili powder
- 1 1/2 tsp cumin, unless you're adding beef, in which case: 1/2 tsp
- 1 tsp coriander
- 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (OR LESS, if you don't like a bit of heat)
- 1 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 heaping tbsp pure cocoa (if not using meat)
- 1 tbsp brown sugar (only if using meat)
- ~2 cups frozen corn kernels
- green onion sliced finely (optional)

Measure out all the spices into a small bowl so they're all ready. If you're still getting used to cooking, chopping your onions before cooking is a good idea. It's also handy to have the cans of tomato already open, and the beans pre-rinsed is a must.

Warm a bit of cooking/olive oil in a pot big enough for all the ingredients. If using beef, brown (cook) the beef on medium high temperature, seasoning with a bit of salt and a good dose of ground pepper. It doesn't have to be cooked through, as you'll be cooking it more in a moment. (You can chop your onions while the meat is browning if you're getting more skilled). Scrape the browned meat into a bowl or a plate and set aside; don't worry about leaving any bits behind in the pot. You may wish to drain some of the beef fat off at this point as well.

Add a little bit more oil in the pot, and turn the heat down to medium. Toss in the onions; they should hiss for a moment. Stir frequently, turning down the heat a little if they're browning quickly at the edges. Cook until they're soft and translucent (sometimes I cook them on a lower temperature for longer to almost start to caramelize them). Add the spices now: don't panic, they will stick to the pot and begin to darken and this is okay. After just a moment or two, turn the heat back up to medium high, then add the tomatoes. The liquid should hiss, and suddenly the browned bits of spice and onion should be easy to scrape up off the bottom of the pot with a wooden utensil or heat proof spatula. (This is basically "[deglazing](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglazing_(cooking)>)" the pan).

I like to cook the tomatoes a little here, but if you like big chunks of tomato, just carry on. Add in the beans and the beef, if using. Add the brown sugar now if using meat (I'm not sure why I just felt it needed a little sweetness). Cook on medium low temp, until the desired consistency is reached, usually at least an hour. If you like it a little runnier, or find it's getting too thick for your liking, you can also add beef stock or water (up to 1 cup). Don't be afraid to taste it to see if it's how you like it!

Once you've got the texture you want, stir in the corn and cook until corn is warmed through. Serve with sour cream (dairy or not) and green onion if desired.
