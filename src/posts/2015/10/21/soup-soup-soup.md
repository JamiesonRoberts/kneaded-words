In good ol' London the weather is hard to predict. It's supposed to be fall right now, but today we've got mid to high teen temperatures (celsius, in case you're American), and yet only last weekend we had sloppy wet flurries that obscured all vision.

None the less, soup has made a reappearance on my weekly menus. So here's three different soups for you to try out.

**Basic Chicken Broth** This one is really plain, but it's great for when you're sick. I freeze the broth in personal sized portions so I don't have to make it while coughing up a storm, but the frozen broth can also then be used in other soup recipes. For a more complex and flavourful chicken soup, I suggest [Smitten Kitchen](http://smittenkitchen.com/blog/2015/01/my-ultimate-chicken-noodle-soup/) (as always).

- one whole chicken (I leave the skin on)
- 2-4 carrots
- 1-2 onions
- 1-3 cloves garlic
- 2-3 celery stalks
- small handful whole peppercorns
- bay leaf
- salt to taste
- 1-3 roma tomatoes (optional; I've also used some older cherry tomatoes I had)

I prefer to prep my vegetables a little beyond a quick rinse: I peel the carrots, onions, and garlic, but leave the celery leaves attached and the tomatoes uncut. This is mostly so that I can fish the carrots out later because I'll add them back into the served broth with the chicken.

Otherwise, just put everything (cleaned but with skins; smash the garlic a bit under the flat of a knife) into a pot big enough to hold it all with a good couple of inches of room at the top. Fill with filtered water to cover ingredients, and bring to a boil. Once boiling, turn down to a simmer -which is just barely bubbling, by the way- and kind of ignore it for at least an hour, maybe two, until the chicken is pretty much falling apart. You're looking to extract all the flavour out of the ingredients into the water.

_Very carefully_ lift the chicken out, trying to keep as much of the liquid in the pot as possible: you may have to do this in pieces if it's really crumbling. Don't worry if some skin or bits get left behind. Remove the carrots and also set aside. Remove the celery, onions, garlic, tomatoes and spices and discard.

Over your next largest pot, bowl or container, set a sieve. Strain broth for any remaining detritus (like chicken skin, peppercorns, or the onion/garlic skins if you left them on). Put it back on to keep warm if you're going to be eating it right away.

Dissect the chicken meat off the bones onto a plate or container. If you're not eating it right away, you can refrigerate the broth and scoop off some of the chicken fat which will solidify as it chills. However, I think the fat adds a bit of flavour and body, so I like to keep it in, especially for when I'm sick and this is lunch or dinner.

To serve, I cook some egg noodles (no name brand is just fine) separately and fill my bowl with them as well as some of the chicken and the carrots before filling with broth. Otherwise, ladle the broth into containers for freezing for later use!

**Squash Soup** Someone told me mine looked more like baby food, but don't worry: you can just personalize it to the texture you want. You'll need a blender for this one: either a glass one, or one that can take heat, or an immersion blender (or "whizzy stick" if you're from my weird family), otherwise you'll have to wait for the soup to cool a little before you can blend it for optimal texture.

- 2-3 strips bacon, cut into small pieces
- 6 cups peeled and cubed butternut squash (about one large or two small)
- 1-2 sweet potatoes, peeled and cubed same size as squash
- 1-2 cooking onions, finely chopped
- 4-6 cups chicken broth
- pinch of rosemary
- salt and pepper to taste
- olive oil (if roasting root veg)

There's three cooking options here, two of which involve pre-roasting the squash and sweet potatoes, one of which does not.

If roasting, you could skip cubing the butternut squash and just roast it in halves. Coat it with some olive oil, season with a little salt and pepper, and roast right on the rack in the oven at 400/425 F until it's tender when poked with a fork. You'll probably want to cube the sweet potato and use a pan to hold them while roasting, however which is part of why I decided to do the following instead:

Trying to achieve a little more roasty flavour from the exposed surface area of cubing both, I tossed the cubed squash and sweet potato in olive oil, some salt and pepper, and spread them out on a rimmed baking sheet to roast (same temp and instructions) until the edges were nice and brown if not starting to blacken.

While roasting, --_or if you aren't, start here_\--: In your soup pot that will fit all ingredients cook the bacon at medium-low heat until it begins to get crispy. Remove with slotted spoon and set aside, leaving the fat in the pan. (I added extra bacon fat I had in reserve). Turn up heat to medium and add onions, cooking until they are translucent. I also kind of caramlized mine by cooking them longer at a lower heat for that yummy flavour.

Add your squash and sweet potato and the pinch of rosemary. Add at least enough broth to just cover the veg. This will create a _very_ thick soup, so add at least the full six cups if you'd like something a bit runnier. Bring to a boil. Turn down to a simmer and cook until veg is falling apart. Cooking time will only be about 20 minutes tops if you roasted the veg already, but maybe as long as an hour if you didn't. You may also need to add more broth (or some water) in the latter case.

If you need to, allow soup to cool, then blend until smooth. If you find the texture too thick, add in more chicken broth. Leaving the soup thick allowed me to save some freezer space when I stored half of my last batch. I'll be adding broth when I thaw it, but since the broth I use is stored at room temp in a tetrapak, I can save precious freezer space.

To serve, simply ladle into bowls, sprinkling with a few crispy bacon bits if you so desire.

**Leek and Fennel Soup with Barley and Bacon** You'd think there'd be a shorter name for that; oh well. This soup will teach you how to use some new ingredients! I know it did for me. The first one is leeks: a super-sized version of green onions, it has a milder taste than onions but just as yummy (if not more so). They seem to be in season at the moment, judging by the quantity and quality in the grocery stores, so I'm super excited. You'll be using the white and light green parts of the leek, so look for ones with the most white when buying. The second is fennel, a bulby-oniony-looking plant with shoots on top and dill-like greens. It has a texture that's a bit like the thick core leaves of cabbage, but smells like liquorice. Don't be put off: I hate liquorice, but fennel bulb cooks up nice and sweet in this soup. You may not have cooked with barley before, but you'll likely have encountered it in a canned or restaurant soup in a similar fashion to this one. This recipe is based off a much shorter version that a reader wrote in to Fine Cooking Magazine.

- 2-4 strips of bacon, chopped into pieces
- 1 extra large clove garlic, minced (chopped really fine)
- one medium-small fennel bulb, chopped into 1cm pieces
- two medium-large leeks, cut in half lengthwise, then sliced (remember, only light green with the white parts)
- 2-3 large carrots, cut into similar sized pieces as the fennel
- 1L chicken broth
- pinch of thyme (~1/4 tsp dried)
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 tbsp pot barley (I've used pearl and it works too).
- salt and pepper to taste

On medium-low heat, cook the bacon until the fat is rendered out and the bacon is brown. Add the garlic and cook for just a moment, then add the fennel, leek and carrot. Season with a bit of salt and pepper and cook until softened. Add broth, thyme, bay leaf and barley and bring to a gentle boil. Turn down to a simmer, and cover for about 10-15 minutes, or until barley and the carrots are tender. Remove bay leaf and it's ready to go! The recipe recommends "finishing" with grated parmesan and a dribble of freshly squeezed lemon juice, but I've never done this. I did try cooking it with a piece of parmesan rind and meeeeeeh, I'm not keen on it.

Enjoy!
