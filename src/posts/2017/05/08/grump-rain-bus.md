_Tuesday Morning_

“Wonder why they took the signs down,” he says robustly, addressing everyone.

_Crickets. But maybe I’ll converse—_

“Aw, fine!” he blusters further, “What? Nobody talks here? Yeah. You’re all deaf and dumb!”

_‘All went lame; all blind’..._ _hm. If you’d just given it a chance_...

_The bus._

He crowds those trying to board, yet lets everyone else go first.

_Shuffling feet. Umbrellas dripping._ _Passes shown, tickets eaten by the machine._

“While the rich are riding in their cars, the poor walk!” grouched the man on the bus.

_Then the poor must have dignity._

He paces back and forth; to the front, to the driver; to the back, to the riders...

_Rain, dripping, swilled around_ _in the crack between window and pane._

“Hmf” pace, pace “Hmf”

_He sits, he stands, he grins._ _No peace?_ _I try to smile mildly._

_I am not deaf and dumb, just choose not to speak._

_What if I were to start a conversation?_ _What would we discuss?_

_The weather. The bus._ _How, at my job, I have to make conversation._

_Is this so much better than silence?_

_Surely my choice not to speak_ _means that I have better things to talk about._

_Don’t I?_
