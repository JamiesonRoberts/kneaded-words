A short story inspired by Ursula Vernon's "[Bad Egg](http://ursulav.deviantart.com/art/Bad-Egg-3192594)"

       *She knows. She knows! But even though she knows, it doesn't matter.*

       _But it does! Because now she's after me._

       _But I'm not scared. I will not be intimidated. Four foot being of pink flesh, clothed in deep red and capped in auburn isn't going to shake me._

       _As such, I sit on my perch and let my knives gleam with satisfaction._

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

The Urban consists of widely spaced buildings and streets which look the same as the next, just as such areas always had in past times. Then, perfection had lain in the beautifully trimmed green lawns, full flower beds and boxes, colorfully painted doors, and clean red bricks. Now, however, the roofs lack shingles in places, revealing the rafters and attics that were once beneath them. The walls rot between rooms, laying bare the inner skeletons of the houses. The red bricks are faded, deteriorating, and chunky beige cement fills holes and gaps. No grass or flowerbeds grace any front lawn, nor are there many trees or bushes. These lawns, robbed of their greenery, are all a dusty sand colour now, which makes up the majority of the Urban's palette. The Teams rage artistic wars on any clear, flat surface they can find, splashing the Urban with foreign-looking sprays of colour. Occasional skirmishes break out.

But the people who live here now don't remember a time when the Urban was anything more–or less, depending on the opinion–than what it is to them now. History is no longer anyone's concern–

Because it no longer applies. Things that would have seemed bizarre in the past happen on a regular basis, and life no longer holds the same sort of qualities it used to...

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

Selena darted, skated, weaving from one shadow to the next. It was generally unnecessary, but it made her feel slick and important. Seven years old and she had been assigned a partner.

She skated further into the heart of the Urban.

Turning a corner she slid into an alcove beneath a rusting tin roof. Two brick walls created a corner beneath it; covered in graffiti. But he was there.

When she saw him, she skid and nearly toppled to her bottom. Trying to comprehend his appearance, one sarcastic, juvenile little eyebrow lifted in curiosity.

A slit opened in the white vastness that was his face and out came sound.

"I work alone," he growled, or as much as something that looked like him could be expected to growl. Selena had to concentrated to keep her eyes from going wide with surprise. She overcompensated and scowled instead.

A slight slick sucking noise sounded and two ebony glass spheres appeared, embedded above the slit that was his mouth. They glowered without focus at the world. He was quite frightening, she decided. Nice. A grin spread across her cracking lips to accompany her scowling eyes.

"Don't laugh at me you little fool." He still seemed to be growling.

"I'm not laughing at _you_, let alone laughing," she retorted. "And I'm not afraid of you either. Come on, punk, we've got an assignment, and if you're not going to lead, I will."

Suddenly he was tumbling towards her at a terrifying speed. Her training kicked in and she was rolling onto one shoulder, out of his path. She felt the wind of his passage by her ankles. Once upright again she turned to watch him race away, and her grin faded.

"Fine," she said to herself. "Fine. I'll do it myself."

And she was off.

Tearing out in the open this time, she raced through the empty streets in broad daylight. At this time of day, not many people left their shelters, except for the select few. Oddly, D-light hours were when the Teams were most active; it showed their cockiness and blatant bravery. Selena, her cohorts, her companions and her enemies were the ones who kept the others from going outside–no one wanted to get caught between Teams. Her anger simmered down as she raced through the streets.

There it was: 420. She slowed her pace and limited herself to the shadows, slick and spy-like once again.

Back against the wall. Look both ways. Turn.

Looking up, she undid her pistol from its holster, aimed, and shot. The cable whispered through the air, silently lodging itself in the brick edge at the top of the wall. Clinging to the gun, she pushed a different button. She zipped upwards.

On the roof, she tied her half-mask over her face and tiptoed up to the fading yellow stretch that was the old EYEGLASSES billboard. It was splashed in Purple and Silver. Not for long, she thought. The Cleveland Bays might be "Royal", but they certainly are not intelligent whatsoever. Two S-cans appeared in one of her hands.

"They think they'll win this. They are so wrong," she muttered. Another silent shot and she was swinging halfway up her canvas, the pistol and cable secure in the harness under her cloak–now both hands would be free. Dangling in the air, she raised her cans...

A force crushed against her entire body, forcing her from the board. Now she really was weightless–flying through the air–straight out past the edge of the building. Bewilderment showed in her eyes alone.

She'd trained for this. No, this would not to stop her.

The cable to her pistol was still intact and attached to her and not the board. With lighting speed she made it retract and shot it again, swinging back towards the billboard.

Her stomach heaved, the cable retracted, freed it from its lodging.

Legs buckling under her landing, she rolled. Now lying on her stomach, she looked up to take in who her attacker was.

Two ebony glass spheres looked down on her once more. In a flash he'd whipped out a pistol of his own and aimed at her. Not doubting his intentions for a second, Selena log-rolled as he fired his first shot.

Into a crouch–standing tall–skating away.

She shot her cable into the wall's edge as she leaped off  of the building.

Once on the ground again she yanked her cable free and kept skating away. What was with this guy? she thought. Even the most competitive of us don't go around shooting members of our own Team!

As she got further away, she slowed gradually, allowing herself time to recoup', now that she was sure he was not in pursuit. Slowly now, she flitted between lengthening shadows, on her way home in the gathering dusk.

A rusty old porthole in a stucco beige wall. Creaky–she ought to oil that. More darkness.

She slid down into the dim light of her crawlspace then fumbled around among her things: Head' had to know what had gone down. In her searching, her hands paused, still hovering over a collection of items. Perhaps there was another solution; a better one. The thoughts in her head swirled like dust.

An impish grin spread from cheek to cheek.

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

_They will regret this._

       _I will strike out on my own! I don't need a Team. I don't need anyone! I can defend my territory completely alone._

       _I am so strong. Capable. Agile. Everything. No. More than everything. If there's no Team, there's no insulting superiors. Pfft. Now if only I could just find a good... That wall. That wall will do quite nicely._

       _And her._

       _Doubtless she will be back; she will return. But,... I will get rid of her. She won't be a problem. No, four foot being of pink flesh, clothed in deep red and capped in auburn. She doesn't scare me._

       _Perhaps my knives need sharpening..._

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

Selena was skating again. She decided that before she would take action against her formerly assigned partner, she would view his work on the billboard. Reaching street 420, she mounted the wall discretely once again.

She cringed as she scraped her knee against the brick barrier around the roof's edge, but was quickly distracted. As the B-board rose into view, so did her anger–what she saw, was not what she wanted to. Her body tensed as her measured steps took her towards the board.

But she was also grinning. Demonically. Ebony-eyes had made a big mistake. And now? Now he would pay.

.               \*               \*               \*               \*

She made quick work of the B-board and surveyed the area as she replaced her S-cans to their home. She had a bird's-eye view of the Urban. Black roofs of shingles, patched with tin or tile, stretched over brick buildings splotched with cement. The occasional stucco wall. All sandy beige and faded red. Rusty, metal doors–

Fwiz!

She dropped to the ground instinctively. The bullet had come somewhere ahead, to her left.

But it wasn't a bullet! She crawled towards the edge of the roof, but, looking back, saw a large blade lodged in the B-board. Reaching her cover, she peeked over.

Three blocks away was the Builder's Wall. Five stories high and just as long, two feet wide across the top; the same faded red brick as everything else. An occasional protruding ledge was present at the base of rectangular gaps in the wall which, at one time, were supposed to contain glass. The wall formed a death-trap U around three blocks. Each Teams' members knew that if you got caught in there, no one was coming to help you. Rumor had it that there were even skulls on some of the inner ledges.

Perched smack dab in the middle was an egg-shaped form of creamy white. Two black limbs dangled below it. Two more folded in front. A sliver of silver was an ornament beside one ebony eye. And below him on the wall? A new Logo.

Selena raised one sarcastic, juvenile little eyebrow.

With a swish of red and a zip of cable she was over the edge and back on the ground. She waited for the thud of the second knife, but it never came.

A confrontation was clearly in order here.

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

"Hey!" she yelled. She came from behind, inside the U, and could see his cream outline through a frame of brick. He didn't seem to hear her. "Hey! You!”

“Egg Head!"

That got his attention. He turned, making a noise like a pig's squeal and was stepping through the frame.

"That's right," she taunted. "You're a big, large, fragile Egg!"

Time paused for them as they watched each other. Her, draped in her crimson cloak, auburn hair flickering in the breeze, below. Him, halfway through the frame, ebony eyes glaring, above. Eyes like black holes, engulfing everything.

Then he was catapulting towards her at top speed. She pivoted in record time and was racing down the streets. Down one, up another, blurring together, all looking the same. A frantic chase with much at stake. Closer, and closer; he was always gaining! Where could she go? She swung around a corner at the last second and yet he still followed with cunning accuracy. The crushing sound of the gravel was almost too

much as–

She peeked out from her hiding place and watched him as he rolled down the street. One of her skates raced ahead, powered by a tiny motor and a computer chip. She hadn't thought he could see while he rolled like that–he had to operate by sound. Teams only know where his ears were though, and even then... Well, perhaps she had out-smarted him. He turned another corner after the noisy mechanized skate. No time to wait and see. With one skate left to roll with, she raced back to the wall.

Without stopping to look this time, she reached the Builder's wall–Ebony Eye's personal Logo above her–and shot her cable into the highest ledge possible. Up she went, and as she did she brought out her S-cans. A flourish and she'd covered most of the Grey and Red. A sloppy job, but she wagered she had very little time until the Egg returned.

And she was right. A roar behind her made her turn just in time to see his Egg-ness hurtling towards her through the air once again. But it was enough of a warning. Her cable propelled her up, swung her in an arch, and with a flick it was free and she was balancing on the very top of the wall.

She only got one step forward before he landed in front of her, blocking her escape. She wobbled precariously from the sudden stop, her gun falling below. She watched as his knife flicked out of Teams-know where. This was the deciding moment.

He lunged.

She skipped away, keeping her balance. His laughter scraped the air; a dried-out cackle.

"You have nothing! Nothing!" he shrieked. "You and the others will fail! I will be the only one left! You can't survive here! It's impos–" He broke into a coughing fit.

In a smear of crimson cloth she was on his back and his one knee buckled. His roar echoed through the U: the sound of a tornado. He thrashed to get her off, but only succeeded in losing his balance and tossing them both off of the wall.

In a final statement as the fighting ex-Team-mates fell, Selena whipped out her last resort. The metal blades whipped together; they weren't very sharp but they would do the job. She ignored her stomach which had dropped past her body, as well as the wind on her face as they fell, and drilled into the back of his head. Out spewed yellow guck and clear pus.

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

_NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!_

       _AH! YOU WILL PAY! YOU WILL ALL PAY!....You'll see... see... you'll.....see...._

\*               \*               \*               \*               \*

His body had cushioned her landing somehow, his white skin,–or was it shell?–cracking into ever more pieces. She rolled off of him, a split second after the impact of their landing and tumbled into a standing position, holding her weapon in front of her, ready to retaliate should he...

But he clearly would not able to attack anyone, anymore.

Selena stared wide-eyed at the muck that was left of him, her body throbbing with uncountable bruises. She began to shudder, scraping off the pus as best she could. But it covered her. Finally fed up, she dashed away, afraid of being caught, still with only one skate.

A battery-powered egg-beater clattered to the cold gravel.

.               \*               \*               \*               \*

In her wash-hole cubby, she threw her last cloth into the pile in the corner opposite her. Her knees up under her chin, arms wrapped around both, and sitting on a threadbare blue towel which was the only barrier between her and the grimy white tiles that made up the room. She stared ahead.

The shudders wouldn't stop coming.
