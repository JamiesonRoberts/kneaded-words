I don't remember what time I started crying.

We'd been at this for hours. I'd just switched her back to the one breast from the other but she was still crying and refusing to latch. I was afraid I didn't have any more milk. That my body had failed us. Failed her.

I put my head in my hands and *wailed*.

I don't know when the husband came from his dish washing in the kitchen. I don't know when one of my hands abandoned my face to slip behind my baby's back and hold her close.

She stopped crying. I didn't.

My husband's breath caught and I ventured a peek out of one eye to the baby below me while my shoulders shook. There she was, doing her damndest to smile at me, though I venture she still had no idea what it meant to smile, her body bouncing along with my jagged breath. I only sobbed harder but she kept trying to smile until my breathing slowed. She turned her now perplexed-looking face to look over my elbow at goodness knows what. And I held her, while my husband held me.
