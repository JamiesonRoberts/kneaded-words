The biggest thing for me, that probably took the longest to realize, is that I thought having a child would slow my life down in certain ways that would add joy to my life. The idea of slowing down to the pace of a child delighted me. Stopping to splash in puddles. Staring in wonder at the sky, or a butterfly, or a bird.

But then the universe handed me a baby who hasn’t wanted to stop moving since she woke up the morning after she was born. If she wasn’t napping on or directly next to my slowly rising and falling chest, she was sleeping in the stroller being bounced gently along the sidewalk. If she was lying down on the floor or in her crib, she was screaming miserably. Sometimes she just screamed at me while on my lap too. (Google purple crying. Lovely stuff.)

Everything is go, go, go. I can’t stop on a walk to admire quirky graffiti or a curious collection of abandoned objects. I have to keep walking. We can’t go to the library and sit and read books. I have to chase her around, stopping her from pulling all the books off the shelves, constantly worried about my abandoned diaper bag’s contents the entire time. I can’t even sit and play with my daughter, as most days she is frenetically jumping from object to object, place to play, every minute or so. She doesn’t even sit still to eat. After five minutes, at most, she’s up and walking around again, yo-yo-ing back every once in a while for another bite since she didn’t actually eat enough to fill her belly.

And all the while I’m trying to come to terms with this reality which is staying home with my daughter, I’m realizing that I really don’t like it. That I miss the intellectual stimulation of just being a damn adult. I mean, I couldn’t even manage cooking for the first four months of her life, if not longer, because if I put her down in anything for longer than a pee-break, she would start wailing.

“It can’t have been that bad.” “You’ll look back on this time fondly.” “You’ve just got to focus on the little joys in life!” “You should be grateful; some people can’t even have children!”

These things echo through my head every day as I struggle to be the happy little mother everyone expects you to be. I _wanted_ this baby. We had our miscarriage. We started at the infertility clinic. I’ve tried so hard to find new joys in life when all of the old ones have been taken away. Do you know what it’s like to no longer be able to do any of the things you enjoyed because there’s a tiny human demanding your attention literally 24/7? Maybe I will look back on this time fondly, but right now it’s hard to see it and it really does seem that bad. Because right now, each struggle we overcome seems to be replaced by something new and just as infuriating.
