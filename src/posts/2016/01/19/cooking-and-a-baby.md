My last post was rather well timed. I gave birth to our little lady, Rowan, only four days later, twenty-four hours after a dramatic water-breaking at the office I was doing some temporary work for.

Let's just say that I haven't seen much of the kitchen, or even grocery stores since then. Just the other day my husband told me he'd cleaned up the kitchen and I replied deadpan "We have a kitchen?" (I also had to coach him through a recipe last night, despite the fact that he was reading it from the magazine I had picked it out of. Don't be that spouse people: learn how to read recipes and the basic skills required to execute them with a modicum of confidence).

In short, having a baby means you make some serious sacrifices when it comes to how you eat. If it weren't for parents constantly buying us groceries and the handful of generous meal-donations from friends, we would have been eating frozen pizzas and carrot sticks exclusively for the first four weeks (although that's almost exactly what we did anyways). Compromises were made, partially because we weren't even having to pay for our groceries so we ended up with prepared bags of salad, already-roasted chickens, and broccoli that was already in florets. But also in what we have bought because prepared things are faster and easier, which means spending a little more money on food. Because a baby requires a lot of attention! And our diet--I mean that strictly in the 'what we are eating' sense-- suffered mightily. At two weeks early and with a limited freezer capacity, I hadn't prepared much in advance.

What *have* we (it's now we) cooked since our bundle of joy arrived? Well, a pot of stew got made the day my waters broke, while we waited for labour to begin. There's been lots and lots of pasta as well as frozen spinach pizzas. Lasagnas, baked mac and cheese, and a shepherd's pie from friends. Lots of smoothies and oatmeal with frozen fruit (no added sugar) for me. My husband managed to mollify the baby for an hour last week so I could cook a batch of mushroom risotto (which wasn't nearly as good as I can normally make it since I was rushing to answer the unhappy baby's cries). There's also been some boneless chicken slathered in BBQ sauce and cooked in the oven--not very exciting stuff. The most interesting item by far is the one I got my husband to cook last night: [a curried chic pea baked sweet potato](http://www.finecooking.com/recipes/sweet-potato-with-coconut-curried-chickpeas.aspx) from Fine Cooking.

The hardest part has been trying to find the time to cook when the baby just wants to nurse and nurse and nurse. Since I'm breastfeeding, this means I'm quite literally strapped to a chair with both hands often engaged in keeping the little one properly attached. Adjusting to her ever changing habits has been a struggle to say the least. If (or when as many tell me) she establishes a pattern, I promise I'll be back on here soon with some more coherent posts that aren't sloppily dashed down while the babe takes a now-rare morning nap. I may post my little one's birth story here as well as rants and thoughts on parenting now that this has become my life, although I'll try to keep it relevant to the food and budgeting as much as possible.

Be back soon!
